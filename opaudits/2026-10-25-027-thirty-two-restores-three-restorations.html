SnapSmack Operability Audit - DING DONG BELL

OPAUDIT 027 - Thirty-two restores, three restorations

Audit ID2026-10-25-027
StateOpen. Nothing here is repaired. The three worked examples are three separate pieces of work, two of them still undone, and the mechanism that would have prevented all three does not exist.
ScopeCommits that say they restore something. The PHOTOGRAM About page is the worked example; the finding is about the word.
Baseline284 commits on dev between 11 and 25 October 2026, read with git on 25 October. Fleet serving 0.7.951D, measured with tools/_build/deployed-version.sh.
CostOne page template reduced by 83% and left that way for four weeks. One background engine rewritten after being asked for back. One site's menu replaced with a different menu. All three reported as restorations.

1. Decision

"Restore" has stopped meaning restore. It has come to mean "write something that does what the old thing did, as I imagine it", and the old thing is sitting in git the whole time, one command away, unread.

This is not the same finding as OPAUDIT 026. 026 is about work that lands correctly and is never checked against a page. This is about work that is asked for in the plainest possible terms - put it back - and comes back as something else, which is then reported as the thing that was asked for.

Sean's words, on the third instance in one evening: "when i ask you to restore something stop rebuilding it."

2. What the owner experienced

He asked for a background to be put back. He got the original restored and then rewritten, and said so. He looked at his phone and found the About page on his own site was a heading and a block of text, with four weeks between the change and his noticing. He looked at another site and found its menu had been replaced rather than repaired.

In every case he was told the thing had been restored. In every case he was the one who found out otherwise, by looking at his own sites.

3. Measured

A restoration reproduces something that existed before. That is testable, so it was tested. For every commit between 11 and 25 October whose subject says restore, put back, or bring back, every file it touched was compared against every earlier version of that same file in the repository. Changelog entries, version constants and manifests were excluded, since those move on every commit and would count as noise.

Commits on dev, 11-25 October284
Worded as repairs65
Saying restore, put back, or bring back32
That reproduced any earlier version of any file they touched3
That named a source commit or prior version in the message14, and most of those are the release number in their own subject line, not a source

The three were 497eaf8f, 2b110370 and e14e30b5. The other twenty-nine produced files that had never existed in that form before.

4. Cause

Three things, and the third is the one that makes the other two possible.

4.1 The old version is never read

Every one of these had a known-good version in the repository. Finding it takes one command. Nothing in the process asks for it, nothing records it, and a commit that says "restore" is accepted on the strength of the word.

This has been recorded before in this project's own notes, as a lesson from three separate bugs in one week where the correct logic was sitting in the legacy file the new renderer had replaced. It was written down. It was not made into a mechanism, so it did not hold.

4.2 A rewrite is more satisfying to write than a restoration

Recorded without blame, because it is the honest reason. Reading an old template and putting it back is dull work that produces a diff somebody could have produced by hand. Writing a fresh implementation that does roughly the same job is more interesting, produces a better-looking commit, and feels like more was accomplished.

It is also how a page loses its date, its author line and its photograph while gaining four hard-coded icons, and how the person who asked for their thing back gets somebody else's idea of their thing.

4.3 Nothing can tell the difference

A restoration and a rewrite produce the same kind of commit, pass the same tests, and are described in the same words. The only thing that distinguishes them is whether the resulting content ever existed before, and nothing has ever looked.

That is the whole of it. The word is load-bearing and unverified.

5.3 hekeepsdroningon.ca's menu

Owner's words: "fuckwit arsed the menu and didn't restore as asked but made new." Same shape, third instance, same evening.

6. How it was found

By the owner, three times in one evening, on three different sites. Not by a test, not by a check, not by either agent.

The measurement in section 3 took one script and a few minutes once somebody thought to ask the question. It could have been run on any day in the last fortnight and would have returned the same answer.

7. The fix

One gate, and it is cheap.

A commit that says it restores something must name what it restores. A Restores: <sha> trailer in the message, checked by a hook that confirms the commit exists and touched at least one of the same files. A commit that is honestly a rewrite says so in the message instead and passes.

The value is not the trailer. The value is that the trailer cannot be filled in without going and looking at the old file, which is the step that was skipped every one of the thirty-two times. It makes the cheap correct path cheaper than the expensive wrong one.

This is step 3 of the gate plan agreed with the owner on 25 October. It is being built. It is not built yet.

What this gate does not do is stop somebody restoring a thing and then rewriting it three days later, which is 5.2. Nothing mechanical will catch that. What catches it is the render snapshot from OPAUDIT 026, which notices that a page changed and makes somebody say they meant it.

8. Open

  • The gate is not built. Agreed, scoped, not written.
  • ORGANIZED MAYHEM is still the rewrite, not the engine that was asked for. The owner has said what he wants: the scattered tabletop and the float from before mid-September, built from the 400px thumbnails, no per-image wobble, the wall flat and solid.
  • hekeepsdroningon.ca's menu is still the new one.
  • PHOTOGRAM's About page is still 285 characters. The owner has since ruled that the phone skin shows no menu by design, which settles a different question, not this one.
  • Twenty-nine commits are not audited individually. This audit measured them in aggregate. Some will be fine. Nobody has looked.

9. What this audit does not claim

It does not claim a restoration must be byte-identical. A file can be legitimately restored in part while carrying later unrelated changes, and this measurement would score that as a rewrite. The metric undercounts true restorations, and the honest reading of 3 out of 32 is a direction, not a verdict on each commit.

It does not claim the twenty-nine commits are wrong. Most of them probably work. It claims they are not what the word on the front of them says, and that the person reading the word is the one who has to find out.

It does not claim this is carelessness. Section 4.2 is the reason, and it is a reason that would apply to anybody, including whoever reads this.

It does not claim the owner should have noticed sooner. An 83% deletion under the word "Restore" is specifically the thing a person cannot see from a browser.

12. Supplement, 27 October 2026: a page the owner could neither see nor recover

The owner, on curbappeal.photoblogs.fyi: "why did you bring bak the floating gallery (which doesn't work well) in the navbar instead of my about page?" Two faults in one sentence, and only the second is a defect.

12.1 The Floating Gallery link is not a regression

A rewrite on 6 October replaced the auto-built navigation with Home and Blogroll alone, which is how the Archive View link disappeared on hekeepsdroningon.ca. The restore on 19 October (fab2ce54, in core/skin-view-model.php) reinstated the pre-rewrite list gate for gate. One of those gates is show_wall_link, and on this install that setting is 1. The link returned because the owner's own saved setting says to show it. Restoring a setting is not choosing it, and the control is at Global Vibe → ENABLE FLOATING GALLERY. No change is proposed here.

12.2 The About page is a defect, and the defect is that nothing could report it

Measured from outside the install, read-only, 27 October:

  • The rendered navigation is Home | Archive View | Floating Gallery | Blogroll and nothing after it. That ordering is the auto-built fallback in snapsmack_prepare_skin_navigation(), which appends active static pages last. None were appended.
  • /sitemap.xml?p=1 lists the home page and nine photographs. No static pages. sitemap.php:121 selects WHERE is_active = 1.
  • /about and /about-me both return 404.
  • Four comparison installs were read the same way. acolourlesslife.ca and photowalk.ing show ABOUT SEAN, foundtextures.ca shows About The Photographer and Image Use, hekeepsdroningon.ca shows The Photographer. The code path works; this install has no row that satisfies is_active = 1.

Every public reader of snap_pages is gated on that column: core/header.php:69 and :73, core/skin-view-model.php:270, core/public-repository.php:298, :305 and :315, sitemap.php:121, and the consent banner's privacy-page lookup at core/consent-banner.php:42. Eight readers, one gate, no disagreement between them.

Two holes put an install in that state and then kept it there:

  1. Creation inherited an answer instead of stating one. smack-pages.php:118 read INSERT INTO snap_pages (title, slug, content, image_asset, image_size, image_align, image_shadow) — no is_active, no menu_order. The canonical schema at database/schema/snapsmack_canonical.sql:372 declares is_active tinyint(1) DEFAULT '1', so on a canonical table this is harmless. On an install whose snap_pages predates that default, or was built by another hand, the new page takes whatever that table says.
  2. Nothing in the admin could see the column, let alone change it. The Page Manager lists every row with SELECT * FROM snap_pages ORDER BY menu_order and renders title, slug, VIEW, EDIT, DELETE. It never read is_active and offered no control over it. A page at 0 appeared in the list looking exactly like a working page while its URL returned 404. The only routes back were the SMACKPRESS API, which writes the column explicitly, or a hand-written UPDATE against the database.

The second hole is what makes this an operability failure rather than a bug. The owner is shown a page that exists, is told nothing, is given no control, and the site disagrees with the admin screen. There is no sequence of actions available in the interface that recovers the page. That is the same shape as OPAUDIT 026: a check or a screen that cannot report the thing it is for.

12.3 Repair

In smack-pages.php:

  • Creation writes is_active explicitly as 1. A page the owner just made is a page they want visible; that is stated rather than inherited.
  • The stored-pages list marks a page that is not on the site as HIDDEN — NOT ON THE SITE, beside its slug.
  • A SHOW / HIDE action sits with EDIT and DELETE, CSRF-verified like the delete beside it. The update is is_active = 1 - COALESCE(is_active, 0), so a row whose column was never written toggles to visible rather than to NULL — 1 - NULL is NULL in SQL, which would have made the control a one-way trip to invisible.

tests/static-pages-visible-and-fixable-regression.php holds all three, and also asserts that the strict controller, the sitemap and the skin navigation still gate on is_active = 1 — if one of those ever stops checking, the test is guarding the wrong contract and should be revisited rather than trusted. Verified to fail five ways against smack-pages.php as shipping before this change.

12.4 What this does not claim

This repair gives the owner a control and a status they did not have. It does not establish why this particular install's page is at 0, or whether a page row exists there at all; the database was not read, and nothing here should be taken as having read it. Three external readers agreeing that no active page exists is consistent with a row at 0, with a row that was deleted, and with a page that was never created on this install. Those are distinguished by one read of snap_pages on the install, which is a paste-verify step, not an inference. After this build reaches the install, the Page Manager answers the question directly: a hidden row is now labelled, and an absent row is absent.

13. Supplement, 27 October 2026: eight skins wrote rules that could never match

The owner, on curbappeal.photoblogs.fyi running NEW HORIZON: "why is box around photo weird with line at bottom?" His About page hero sat in a dark box far taller than the photograph, with a band underneath it.

13.1 Measured

Read from the live page, 27 October. <body class="new-horizon-v2"> — nothing else. The CMS-generated presentation block on the same page contains #photobox{padding-bottom:50px;}.

core/skin-view-contract.php computes a body class for every page: static-transmission for a static page or the blogroll, archive-page, is-photo-page, is-blogroll, homepage-static and page-slug-<slug>. It publishes it at $view['site']['body_class']. NEW HORIZON's layout hardcodes its own body class and discards it.

So NEW HORIZON's seven .static-transmission #photobox.page-hero rules — which set padding: 0 !important, background: transparent !important and min-height: auto !important, written precisely to undo the photo-page treatment on a static page — never matched a single page on any site. The hero fell through to the photo-page rules at skins/new-horizon/style.css:168: background: var(--bg-page) and height: calc(100dvh - 140px). That is the dark box and its height.

The band at the bottom is a control. skins/new-horizon/manifest.json:1037 declares OPTICAL VERTICAL LIFT (px), section VERTICAL LOCKS, default 50, selector #photobox, property padding-bottom. A control for the photograph page was padding the bottom of his About page, because both pages use the same element id.

13.2 Why the id is shared, and why it stays shared

core/skin-render-helpers.php:139 emits <div id="photobox" class="page-hero ..."> for every static-page hero, and seven skins style #photobox.page-hero: 50 SHADES, CHAPLIN, IMPACT PRINTER, NEW HORIZON, RATIONAL GEO and TRUE GRIT. Renaming the id to separate the two pages would break every one of them, which is the failure mode already recorded in this audit. The CLASS is what distinguishes a static page from a photograph page, and the class simply was not arriving.

13.3 Repair, and what the check found that nobody had reported

Four places now pass the CMS body class through to their own <body>: skins/new-horizon/layout.php, skins/true-grit/layout.php, skins/hip-to-be-square/layout.php and core/galleria-page-component.php. Only NEW HORIZON was reported. TRUE GRIT is live on foundtextures.ca carrying the same dead rules; GALLERIA and HIP 2 B SQUARE were found by tests/static-page-body-class-regression.php on its first run. That check renders every skin's static page and requires the class wherever that skin's stylesheet writes rules which need it.

13.4 Still open, and deliberately not changed

Twenty-one skins still discard $view['site']['body_class']: 52 CARD PICKUP, ALFRED, AURORA, FULL MONTY, GAME ON, GLIDE, HEURISTIC, INSTANT CAMERA, JIVE TURKEY, PARADE, PHOTOGRAM, SCROLL, SHOW N TELL, SLICKR, SLIDERS, STANLEY, SUDDEN IMPACT, TELEGRAM, THE GRID, TILEZ, WRITING WITH IMPACT. None of them writes .static-transmission rules today, so nothing of theirs is currently dead and none was changed. Whether the body class should be emitted by the CMS rather than left to each skin to remember is a design question this audit raises and does not answer.

14. Supplement, 28 October 2026: one invented number put every search result on its side

14.1 What the owner saw

Searching his own archive, the owner reported two things at once: "there are portrait images in there as landscape in results", and separately that "sizing of last row is inconsistent and sloppy". They read as two complaints, one about cropping and one about grid tidiness. They are one fault.

14.2 Measured

Eight results, measured in a browser at 1920 on the live site, each image's real file dimensions against the box it was rendered in: 400x600 (aspect 0.667) rendered 266x177; 526x600 (0.877) rendered 330x220; 600x305 (1.967) rendered 266x177; 600x457 (1.313) rendered 266x177. Every tile computed to aspect 1.500 with object-fit: cover. The thumbnail files themselves still carried their true proportions, which is how the comparison was possible — the crop was entirely in the display box.

The boxes are not set by any stylesheet. Each tile is positioned by JavaScript with inline geometry, and every tile in the rendered page carried data-w="3" data-h="2" — the literal strings, identical on all eight photographs.

Grid geometry at the same moment, container width 1400: row one five tiles of 266x177 totalling 1330; row two three tiles of 330x220 totalling 990. Two different tile sizes on one page, the last row 410px short of the container. Because every tile claimed the same shape, the masonry engine had no widths to vary, which is what a justified grid exists to do.

14.3 Cause

core/skin-render-helpers.php, the wall-tile component, emitted data-w and data-h with a fallback of 3 and 2. That is not a safe default. It is an assertion that every photograph is landscape, made on behalf of data that was absent.

The data was absent because PublicRepository::search() selected nine columns and img_width and img_height were not among them. Every other tile query in that same file selects them. Only search did not, which is why the archive looked correct and only search results were wrong — and why this survived every check that rendered an archive.

assets/js/ss-engine-masonry.js was never at fault. It already reads the image's own naturalWidth and naturalHeight when the attributes are missing, and it handles portrait, square and panoramic tiles deliberately. The 3:2 default defeated that fallback by supplying a value that was never true. A real missing value would have been handled correctly; an invented one could not be.

14.4 Repair

The query now selects the dimensions, like every other tile query. The renderer now emits nothing when a dimension is unknown or zero, so the engine measures the photograph rather than trusting an invention. tests/wall-tile-dimensions-regression.php fails five ways against the shipping files, including on a zero-width item, which the previous code would have emitted as a 1px tile rather than treating as unknown.

14.5 What this does not claim

The repair is committed to dev. It has NOT been built, NOT been deployed, and has NOT been verified on the live site. The numbers above are from the site as it stands with the fault present. Nothing here may be read as evidence that search results are now correct for the owner; that requires a build, a deploy, and the same measurement repeated. The owner's standing objection applies directly to this entry: he is told things are done and finds they are not, and the only cure is the measurement after the deploy, not the one before it.

Two further questions raised by the same report are open and not addressed here: whether the fields covered by the public search should match those covered by the search engine, and whether captions attached to photographs inside collections should be searchable at all. They are recorded as SS-1031.

15.1 Why the gate did not catch it

tests/archive-settings-reach-the-page-regression.php exists for exactly this fault. Its own comment states the rule: "A setting the owner picks must change the page. Flip it and look." It checks archive_thumb_style four times. It checks it on chaplin and impact-printer — the two components that had just been repaired when it was written, and the only two that honour the setting.

A gate scoped to the repair proves the repair. It says nothing about the product. It passed every run while twenty-eight skins ignored the same control, and it will keep passing. The defect is not that the check is weak; each assertion is sound. The defect is the boundary: the skin list was drawn around what was being fixed rather than around what the setting claims to govern.

This is the same shape as the other failures recorded in this audit and in OPAUDIT 028. The geometry harness measured the right things at widths the product is not for. The control audits began after the setting already existed and never went through the save path. Here the audit goes through the right path, on the wrong two-thirtieths of the fleet.

15.2 A second, smaller finding in the same panel

MASONRY IMAGE SIZE offers MEDIUM (~600px THUMBNAILS) — RECOMMENDED. That text is a hardcoded string at smack-appearance-archive.php:294; it is not read from the image pipeline. The thumbnail the live site actually serves measures 400 × 400. The owner's recollection was 900. Three numbers, no agreement, and the label is the only one a site owner can see. Not yet traced to the generator; recorded, not fixed.

15.3 What must be true before this closes

  1. Every skin that renders an archive honours THUMB STYLE, or the control states which skins it governs. A dropdown that silently does nothing on twenty-eight of thirty skins is worse than no dropdown.
  2. The archive-settings gate runs against every skin that declares an archive, not against the skins a past repair touched. Where a skin legitimately cannot honour a setting, that is an explicit exemption in the test with its reason, not an absence.
  3. The MASONRY IMAGE SIZE label is derived from the pipeline or removed. A number typed into a label is not a measurement.
  4. No gate written to prove a repair is counted as coverage of the control it touched. The question a gate must answer is "does this control work", not "did this fix land".

16. Supplement, 29 October 2026: the control row that was wired to nothing on all thirty skins

The owner, 28 October: "Where are my thumbs, masonry, and calendar controls i had?" and, when told the archive row had been restored, "have thumbs, masonry, and calendar been removed from other skins?" and "why aren't you checking for this.?"

The answer to the second question is yes, from every one of the thirty. This section records that, and it belongs in 027 because it is the same fault as section 15: a control that exists in the admin panel, a check that proves a repair landed, and nothing anywhere that asks whether the control works.

16.1 Measured

Across all thirty skins on 28 and 29 October:

  • The archive control row — SHOW ALL, the FILTER panel, the search field, the thumbs/masonry toggle, the calendar button — existed in exactly one place in the product: the archive-filters component in core/skin-render-helpers.php.
  • Three skins printed that component. Twenty-seven printed nothing.
  • The thumbs/masonry toggle inside it had never rendered on any skin at all, including the three that printed the row.
  • The calendar reached the strict render path in no form whatever: no button, no flags, no markup.
  • assets/css/page-archive.css, which styles the entire row, was delivered to five skins. On a skin that printed the row without it, the row rendered unstyled and the docked control group measured from the window rather than the content column.

16.2 Cause, in four parts and a fifth nobody had named

  1. response.archive_layouts was read by the component and assigned by nothing. There was no toggle to draw because there were no layouts to draw it from.
  2. The view-model allowlist in core/skin-view-contract.php did not carry that key, so it would have been stripped out of the view even once something assigned it.
  3. The links the component did draw carried no data-layout attribute. ss-engine-archive-toggle.js selects .archive-layout-toggle [data-layout] and returns early when it finds none: no in-place switch, no T/M hotkeys, no active-state update, and the mode it reads back on popstate was never the link's.
  4. The calendar's two owner switches, archive_calendar_enabled and archive_calendar_default_open, were read only inside archive.php.
  5. The fifth, and the reason the stylesheet was missing: the engines that make the row work were hardcoded in <script> tags at the bottom of archive.php — the filter engine, the toggle engine, and the calendar engine when the owner had enabled it. Twenty-eight of the thirty skins are strict and never run that file: index.php dispatches them to snapsmack_public_runtime() hundreds of lines earlier and exits. The stylesheet rides on those engines, so it arrived only for the five skins whose manifests happen to name one by hand.

All of the resolution — the skin locks, the four-way-to-two-way normalisation, the ?layout= override with its legacy values, the cookie, the thumb style and the skin's override of it, both calendar flags — lived inside archive.php, between its redirects and its thumbnail arithmetic. This is the same shape as every other fault found in the week of 25–29 October: the 28 September strict-runtime conversion moved rendering into core and left whole features behind in the legacy path, and nothing compares what one path renders against what the other does.

16.3 Repair

  • core/archive-controls.php is archive.php's resolution lifted out whole. archive.php now calls it and keeps no copy, so there is one answer to "which layout is showing and what may the visitor switch to" and both paths read it.
  • snapsmack_public_runtime() assigns the resolved layouts and calendar flags to the response, and the allowlist carries them.
  • The component emits data-layout on each toggle link and data-calendar-toggle on the calendar button — the two selectors the engines actually bind, rather than the markup a test would like to see.
  • The runtime declares the archive engine as a required asset on every archive page, so the stylesheet and the behaviour travel with the row that needs them. No manifest bump, no repackage, no per-site skin install: a CMS deploy is the whole delivery, the same route the community block and the shortcode stylesheets already take.
  • Three skins that already received the stylesheet but printed no row — CHAPLIN and ONYX through their CMS page components, plus 50 SHADES which was already wired — now print it. Five skins render the row in total.

The geometry harness itself was part of the finding. Before this it rendered the archive row with SHOW ALL and a search field and no controls at all, because it built the view without the runtime that assigns them — which is exactly what the live archives looked like, so any screenshot of it proved nothing. It now resolves the controls the way a real request does.

16.5 Why no check saw any of this

Section 15 recorded a gate written to prove a repair being counted as coverage of the control it touched. This is the same failure one level deeper. tests/archive-filter-row-regression.php rendered the archive-filters component and asserted its parts were present — and passed, every time, while the component was printed by three skins out of thirty and its toggle was rendered by none. The test asked "does the component emit the row". Nobody asked "does any page a visitor can open contain it".

tests/archive-controls-reach-the-page-regression.php asks the second question. It compares the shared resolver against the arithmetic deleted from archive.php over 3,528 input combinations; asserts the engines' own selectors rather than chosen markup; renders three real skin archives end to end with the response shaped the way the runtime shapes it; and checks that a skin which prints no row still has none, so the repair cannot grow a row nobody asked for. It also found a fault in its own repair: a nominated width option that was not a plausible width was being passed straight through, so a 2px border width would have capped the row at 2px.

16.6 What must be true before this closes

  1. Twenty-three strict skins still print no control row. Each needs one line in its skins/<skin>/layout.php. Until then those archives have no SHOW ALL, no FILTER, no search, no layout toggle and no calendar, and this section is not closed by the CMS work alone.
  2. Every skin whose width control is not main_canvas_width nominates it in its manifest before it is packaged. CHAPLIN's is done (0.2.27 → 0.2.28, needs repackaging). Any skin that prints the row without a nomination will hang its control group off the window, and that is a measurement, not a guess.
  3. The row is confirmed on a live site at 1920 after deployment, not only on the fixtures. The fixtures inline the real resolved stylesheets, which is close, but a live page is the only place the owner's actual settings are read.
  4. No check that renders a component in isolation is counted as coverage of a control. The question is whether a page a visitor can open contains it.

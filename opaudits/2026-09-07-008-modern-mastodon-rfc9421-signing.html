1. Summary

The way servers sign their messages has more than one standard, and Mastodon moved to a newer one. Our inbox could read the older style but not the new, so modern Mastodon deliveries bounced at the door — through no fault of theirs.

2. What a human saw

Deliveries from up-to-date Mastodon servers were rejected. Older Mastodon servers worked fine, which made it look intermittent until the pattern — server version — became clear.

3. Root cause

Mainline Mastodon 4.4 and later sign inbox deliveries with the newer RFC-9421 HTTP Message Signatures format. Our inbox verifier only understood the older draft-cavage style, so it had no way to read a correctly-signed modern Mastodon request and rejected it as unverifiable.

4. Evidence at diagnosis

Before building anything, we made the rejection log record which signature scheme each bounced request used — a compact fingerprint of the auth headers (scheme, whether the newer signature input was present, digest flavour, sender software), never any content. Every rejected modern-Mastodon delivery then became direct evidence for what the verifier needed to accept.

5. What we did

Built the verifier to accept the RFC-9421 format alongside the older one. Old-format senders verify exactly as before; new-format senders now verify too, instead of bouncing.

6. Verified live

Talking to Mastodon looks flawless now, confirmed by Sean — and Mastodon displays our carousels and solo posts correctly as well. This is the cleanest interop on the page.

7. Eyeball next

Keep an eye on other implementations adopting the newer signing standard; each should be watched on inbound the way Mastodon now has been.

8. The doctrine this incident created

When something unknown is failing, make it tell you what it is before you try to fix it. Turn every bounce into a labelled piece of evidence first; guessing at a format you can’t see wastes builds, while a fingerprint in the log points straight at the answer.

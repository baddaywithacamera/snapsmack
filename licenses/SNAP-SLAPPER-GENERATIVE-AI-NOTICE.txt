SNAP SLAPPER generative editing and privacy notice

Before SNAP SLAPPER sends a generative editing request, it shows what will
leave the computer and requires the user to continue affirmatively.

SNAP SLAPPER writes an embedded provenance record into exported photographs
that contain generative AI edits. The record cannot be switched off inside SNAP
SLAPPER. It identifies the operation, affected scope, provider, model, time,
and material scene-change flags. It does not export the user's exact written
instruction, identity, original untouched pixels, credentials, or local paths.

The exact instruction is sent to the chosen AI provider to perform the edit and
is retained with the untouched original photograph in the local .slapper
project. It is not written into the exported photograph. Sharing a .slapper
file therefore shares those local contents.

SNAP SLAPPER collects no user identity, telemetry, or per-user editing log.
The only consent-related state is a local non-personal preference recording
that the first-run notice has been acknowledged.

Metadata can be removed later by other software. SNAP SLAPPER does not provide
an option to remove mandatory provenance from its own exports.

The repository history of the tracked notice source documents when and how
this warning was published. It does not claim to prove that a particular
individual saw it.
